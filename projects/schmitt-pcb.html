Schmitt Trigger PCB

Five Altium revisions of the same circuit, over a summer, for no class and no credit. The point was never the Schmitt trigger.

Role
Solo, self-directed
Tools
Altium Designer, Fusion 360
Revisions
5
Assigned by
Nobody

WhyTeaching myself the tool nobody had taught me.

No course required this. I'd done plenty of circuit analysis on paper and in simulation, and none of it had ever forced me to answer the question a fabrication house asks: can this actually be built?

So over a summer I taught myself Altium Designer and Fusion 360 from nothing, and picked a circuit simple enough that the circuit wouldn't be the hard part.

The circuitPositive feedback, on purpose.

A Schmitt trigger is a comparator with hysteresis — two thresholds instead of one. It's built by feeding the output back to the non-inverting input, which is the opposite of what you spend an entire analog course learning to do.

That positive feedback is the whole trick. Once the output starts moving it drives itself further in the same direction, which snaps the transition and shifts the threshold away from the input. The result is a comparator that doesn't chatter on a noisy or slow-moving signal — the input has to travel a real distance to flip it back.

Negative feedback makes an amplifier behave. Positive feedback makes it decide.

Five revisionsWhat actually changed.

The schematic barely moved across the five boards. Almost everything I learned was in the layout — footprints that didn't match real parts, routing that passed DRC and would still have been miserable to assemble, and the slow realisation that a board is a mechanical object as much as an electrical one.

OutcomeThe skill, not the board.

The board itself is unremarkable and I'd say so in an interview. What it bought me was fluency: schematic capture, footprint libraries, layout, DRC, Gerber export — the boring pipeline that stands between a working circuit and a physical object.

Every board I've designed since — the water bottle's front end, the bench instrument — started from what these five taught me.
