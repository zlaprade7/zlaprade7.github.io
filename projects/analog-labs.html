Analog design

Differential pairs, cascodes, output stages.

Differential pairs and common-mode rejection, cascode stages, MOSFET and op-amp behavior, push-pull output stages. Everything was simulated first, then built and measured on the bench. The most useful habit I picked up was asking where noise enters a circuit and what rejects it. That's why the water bottle uses an instrumentation amplifier instead of a bare ADC read, and why the multitool bench's output devices sit inside a feedback loop.

Measurement

Turning a resistance into an angle, with error bars.

One lab I liked: measure a potentiometer's shaft angle by using it to set the frequency of a 555 oscillator, then read that frequency on a microcontroller with interrupt-based period averaging. Resistance becomes frequency, frequency becomes a timestamp difference, and that becomes an angle.

The transfer function comes from the 555's internals: the capacitor always charges and discharges through exactly ln(2) time constants, because the internal comparators trip at one third and two thirds of the supply. That's where the familiar 1.44 factor comes from, it's 1 over ln(2).

The uncertainty analysis taught me the most. Propagating component tolerances through the transfer function showed that the capacitor dominates the error budget, and that the total uncertainty isn't constant across the range: it runs from about 1.7% at one end to 4% at the other, because sensitivity scales with 1/f². The measurement is noticeably worse at one end of the sweep, and nothing in the schematic tells you that.
