Wearable Sensor Glove

Showing only the projects that worked would give a false account of how engineering actually proceeds. This is the one I am in the middle of.

If you want to ask me hard questions about a problem I have not solved, this is where to start.

ProposalPut the sensors on the operator.

The lab needs a way to drive a robotic hand. The established approach uses a physical linkage the operator manipulates, which is accurate but bulky and tethers the operator to a rig.

I proposed the inverse: instrument the operator instead. A glove that reads what the hand is already doing and mirrors it onto the robot in real time.

  • BNO055 at the wrist. A 9-DOF sensor with onboard fusion, so it outputs absolute orientation rather than raw rates requiring integration.
  • Five resistive flex sensors, one per finger, each in a voltage divider feeding an ADC channel.
  • ESP32 to ESP32 over ESP-NOW. Peer to peer, no router, no PC in the loop. The receiving board drives the servos directly.
Flex sensors are the worse sensor, and I chose them first on purpose. Per-finger IMUs would be more accurate, but they would mean I²C multiplexing and five simultaneous fusion problems before I know whether the concept works at all. Flex sensors are a voltage divider and an analogRead. They let me prove the system end to end and then earn the right to make it precise.

Open problemsWhat remains unsolved.

  • Flex sensor calibration. Each sensor has a different flat and bent resistance, and those values drift with wear. A per-sensor calibration routine is not optional.
  • Mapping bend to servo angle. Finger curl is not linear in resistance, and a human finger's range does not correspond to a servo's.

NextOne finger before five.

Get a single finger tracking cleanly end to end before scaling. Measure latency and tracking fidelity properly rather than assessing them by eye. Then determine whether flex sensors are sufficient, or whether the accuracy requirement forces the upgrade to per-finger IMUs.
