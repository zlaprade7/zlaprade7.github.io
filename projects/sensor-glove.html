Wearable Sensor Glove

A wireless teleoperation glove for a university robotics lab. My proposal, and I'm building it now. It isn't finished, and it's listed here as unfinished.

If you want to ask me hard questions about something I haven't solved yet, this is the one.

The ideaTeleoperation you wear.

The lab needs a way to drive a robotic hand. The usual approach uses a physical linkage the operator moves, which is accurate but bulky and ties you to a rig.

I proposed putting the sensors on the operator instead. A glove that reads what your hand is already doing and mirrors it onto the robot in real time. You move freely, the hand follows.

  • BNO055 at the wrist. A 9-DOF sensor with fusion on board, so it gives me absolute orientation instead of raw rates to integrate.
  • Five flex sensors, one per finger, each in a voltage divider into an ADC channel. Bend changes resistance, resistance changes voltage.
  • ESP32 to ESP32 over ESP-NOW. Peer to peer, no router, no PC in the loop. The receiving board drives the servos directly.
Why flex sensors first, when they're the worse option. Per-finger IMUs would be more accurate, but that means I²C multiplexing and five simultaneous fusion problems before I know whether the concept even works. Flex sensors are a voltage divider and an analogRead. They let me prove the system end to end and then earn the right to make it precise.

Open problemsWhat I haven't solved.

  • Flex sensor calibration. Every sensor has a different flat and bent resistance, and they drift as they wear. A per-sensor calibration routine isn't optional.
  • Mapping bend to servo angle. Finger curl isn't linear in resistance, and a human finger's range doesn't match a servo's.
