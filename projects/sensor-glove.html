The ideaTeleoperation you wear.

The lab needs a way to drive a robotic hand. The established approach uses a physical linkage the operator manipulates — accurate, but bulky, and it tethers you to a rig.

I proposed the opposite: put the sensors on the operator. A glove that reads what your hand is already doing, and mirrors it onto the robot in real time. You move freely; the hand follows.

  • BNO055 IMU at the wrist — a 9-DOF sensor with onboard fusion, so it outputs absolute orientation directly instead of handing me raw rates to integrate.
  • Five resistive flex sensors, one per finger, each in a voltage divider into an ADC channel. Bend changes resistance; resistance changes voltage.
  • ESP32 → ESP32 over ESP-NOW — peer-to-peer, no router, no PC in the loop. The receiving board drives the servos directly. The whole system is self-contained.
Why flex sensors first: they're the crude option. Per-finger IMUs would be more accurate, but they'd mean I²C multiplexing and five simultaneous fusion problems before I know whether the concept works at all. Flex sensors are a voltage divider and an analogRead. They let me prove the system end-to-end and then earn the right to make it precise. Choosing the worse sensor on purpose, to de-risk the thing you don't yet understand, is a deliberate call — not a shortcut.

NextWhere it goes.

Get one finger tracking cleanly end-to-end before scaling to five. Measure latency and tracking fidelity properly rather than eyeballing it. Then decide whether flex sensors are good enough, or whether the accuracy problem forces the upgrade to per-finger IMUs.

The interesting question isn't whether it works. It's how much precision the cheap sensor buys you before you have to pay for the expensive one.
