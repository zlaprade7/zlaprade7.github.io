WearableSensor Glove

A wireless teleoperation glove for a university robotics lab. I proposed it and I'm building it with the team. It isn't finished, and I'm listing it anyway.

5
flex sensors
9-DOF
wrist IMU
2
boards, linked directly

Problem

The lab needs a way to drive a robotic hand, and the current rig ties the operator to a desk.

The standard approach uses a physical linkage the operator moves. It's accurate, but bulky. I proposed doing it the other way around: put the sensors on the operator. A glove that reads what the hand is already doing and mirrors it onto the robot in real time.

Plan

Sensors on the hand and a direct radio link, starting with the simpler sensor on purpose.

A 9-DOF IMU at the wrist gives absolute orientation with onboard fusion, so there are no raw rates to integrate. Five resistive flex sensors, one per finger, each sit in a voltage divider feeding an ADC channel. Two microcontrollers talk directly over ESP-NOW with no router or PC in between, and the receiving board drives the servos.

Flex sensors are the less accurate option, and I chose them first on purpose. Per-finger IMUs would mean I²C multiplexing and five fusion problems before I even know whether the concept works. Flex sensors are just a voltage divider and an analog read, which lets me prove the system end to end first and improve the accuracy later.

Trials & Errors

What's still unsolved.

  • Flex sensor calibration. Each sensor has a different flat and bent resistance, and those values drift with wear. A per-sensor calibration routine is not optional.
  • Mapping bend to servo angle. Finger curl is not linear in resistance, and a human finger's range does not correspond to a servo's.

Where it stands. Still in build, with both problems above still open. Nothing here has been measured yet, so anything I said about how well it works would be a guess.

Result

No results yet. Here's the plan.

One finger before five: get a single finger tracking cleanly end to end, measure latency and accuracy properly instead of judging by eye, and then decide whether flex sensors are good enough or the design needs per-finger IMUs.
